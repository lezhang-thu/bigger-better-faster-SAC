# Maximization Target for TD Learning

Consider a replay transition beginning at $(s_t,a_t)$ and an $n$-step trajectory segment. Let $Q_\theta$ be the learned action-value function and let $V_{\bar\theta}^{\pi}$ be the target-network value under the target policy $\pi$.

## Candidate targets

The one-step target is

$$
Y_t^{(1)}=r_{t+1}+\gamma V_{\bar\theta}^{\pi}(s_{t+1}).
$$

The uncorrected $n$-step target is

$$
Y_t^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^k r_{t+k+1}+\gamma^n V_{\bar\theta}^{\pi}(s_{t+n}).
$$

Because the intermediate actions in the replayed trajectory were generated by a behavior policy, $Y_t^{(n)}$ can be biased for the current target policy when the two policies are misaligned.

The proposed final target selects the larger candidate:

$$
Y_t^{\max}=\max\{Y_t^{(1)},Y_t^{(n)}\}.
$$

Equivalently, using $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$,

$$
Y_t^{\max}=Y_t^{(1)}+[Y_t^{(n)}-Y_t^{(1)}]_+.
$$

Thus, the multi-step trajectory can raise the target above the one-step target, but it cannot lower it.

## TD error and PER priority

The resulting TD error is

$$
\delta_t^{\max}=Y_t^{\max}-Q_\theta(s_t,a_t)
=Y_t^{(1)}-Q_\theta(s_t,a_t)+[Y_t^{(n)}-Y_t^{(1)}]_+.
$$

A conventional prioritized experience replay score is

$$
u_t=|\delta_t^{\max}|+\varepsilon,
$$

with sampling priority

$$
P(t)=\frac{u_t^\alpha}{\sum_j u_j^\alpha}.
$$

## Intended behavior

- For a high-reward replay trajectory, one typically expects $Y_t^{(n)}>Y_t^{(1)}$. The method then uses $Y_t^{(n)}$, allowing the rare reward to propagate over multiple steps and giving the transition a potentially larger PER priority.
- For a low-reward or stale replay trajectory, one typically expects $Y_t^{(n)}\le Y_t^{(1)}$. The method then falls back to $Y_t^{(1)}$, so the unfavorable behavior-policy suffix creates neither an additional negative multi-step correction nor an additional priority increase. The ordinary one-step TD error can still be large when the critic itself needs correction.

## Interpretation and main risk

The one-step target is policy-consistent because it bootstraps immediately under the target policy and does not depend on a sequence of old behavior actions. It is not perfectly unbiased for the true $Q^\pi$ under function approximation: target-network lag, value approximation error, and policy changes remain. With an exact bootstrap value for a fixed policy, it is unbiased in expectation; in practice, it is better viewed as a sample of an approximate policy-consistent Bellman backup.

The main risk is upward, or maximization, bias. Taking the maximum preferentially selects optimistic estimation noise and favorable stochastic outcomes. Repeated replay can therefore inflate $Q$-values, especially in stochastic environments or when rare lucky trajectories receive high priority. The method should consequently be understood as an intentionally optimistic backup for sparse-reward learning, not as an unbiased off-policy correction.
