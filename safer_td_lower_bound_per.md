# Safer One-Step TD Learning with a Multi-Step Lower-Bound Signal and PER

Consider a transition starting at $(s_t,a_t)$, with discount factor $\gamma$, target policy $\pi$, and target-network value estimate $V_{\bar\theta}^{\pi}$.

## Targets

The one-step TD target is

$$
Y_t^{(1)}=r_{t+1}+\gamma V_{\bar\theta}^{\pi}(s_{t+1}).
$$

The uncorrected $n$-step target from the sampled trajectory is

$$
Y_t^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^k r_{t+k+1}+\gamma^n V_{\bar\theta}^{\pi}(s_{t+n}).
$$

With the expected-backup setting used by ablation 29, both occurrences of
$V_{\bar\theta}^{\pi}$ use the exact policy-weighted mixture of the target
critic's per-action C51 distributions before projection. In particular, the
$n$-step endpoint does not sample a bootstrap action; this avoids feeding
endpoint action-sampling variance through the one-sided signal below.

Because the sampled trajectory may have been generated by a behavior policy different from $\pi$, $Y_t^{(n)}$ is not used as the sole TD target. Instead, it supplies only a positive lower-bound auxiliary signal:

$$
h_t^{\mathrm{LB}}=\left[Y_t^{(n)}-Q_\theta(s_t,a_t)\right]_+,
\qquad [x]_+\equiv\max(x,0).
$$

Thus, the multi-step return can raise an underestimated action value but cannot push it downward.

## Critic objective

Let

$$
\delta_t^{(1)}=Y_t^{(1)}-Q_\theta(s_t,a_t).
$$

The critic loss is

$$
\mathcal L_Q=\ell\!\left(\delta_t^{(1)}\right)
+\lambda_{\mathrm{LB}}\ell\!\left(h_t^{\mathrm{LB}}\right).
$$

The recommended default is

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{\mathrm{LB}}=0.1}.
$$

## Prioritized experience replay

Use both the one-step TD error and the positive lower-bound signal to define the transition's priority score:

$$
u_t=\left|\delta_t^{(1)}\right|+\eta h_t^{\mathrm{LB}}+\varepsilon,
$$

where $\varepsilon>0$ ensures that every transition retains nonzero priority. The recommended default is

$$
\boxed{\eta=0.5}.
$$

For replay buffer $\mathcal D$, the sampling probability is

$$
P(t)=\frac{u_t^{\alpha}}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal D}u_j^{\alpha}},
$$

where $\alpha$ controls the strength of prioritization.

The PER importance-sampling weight is

$$
w_t=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal D|P(t)}\right)^{\beta},
$$

typically normalized within each minibatch as

$$
\tilde w_t=\frac{w_t}{\max_{j\in\mathcal B}w_j}.
$$

The weighted critic objective over a sampled minibatch $\mathcal B$ is therefore

$$
\mathcal L_Q^{\mathrm{PER}}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B}\tilde w_t
\left[\ell\!\left(\delta_t^{(1)}\right)
+\lambda_{\mathrm{LB}}\ell\!\left(h_t^{\mathrm{LB}}\right)\right].
$$

This formulation keeps one-step TD learning as the primary objective while allowing unusually successful sampled trajectories to contribute an additional upward learning signal and receive greater replay priority.
